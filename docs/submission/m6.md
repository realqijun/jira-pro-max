# Milestone 6 - Monetization and pricing strategy

The current release is a free research preview, and the landing page says so.
This removes friction while we validate whether the Evidence-to-Decision workflow solves a sufficiently painful problem.
No checkout is implied.

## Proposed pricing

| Tier       |     Indicative price | Intended customer                          | Scope                                                                                                 |
| ---------- | -------------------: | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Preview    |                 Free | Individual evaluators and small Projects   | Core Project management, 3 Projects, Assistant at 50 turns a day, Proposals, Decision Memory, BYO key |
| Team       | US$12 per user/month | Small professional teams                   | Unlimited Projects, shared Projects, higher Assistant and Render limits, templates, MCP               |
| Business   | US$25 per user/month | Multiple teams                             | Organization workspaces, permissions, audit export, integrations and priority support                 |
| Enterprise |               Custom | Organizations with compliance requirements | SSO, retention controls, dedicated limits, vendor-direct or private model deployment                  |

Prices are hypotheses to test with early users, not a finalized commercial plan.

## What it costs us to serve a User

Measured on current `main` on 30 September 2026 with `gpt-4o-mini`, the model the deployment runs, through OpenAI directly.
Token counts come from each API response; costs are those tokens at OpenAI's list price.
Method, per-run numbers and commands: [artifacts/cost-2026-09-30](../../artifacts/cost-2026-09-30/README.md).

| Unit                                                | Tokens (prompt / completion)       | Cost                         | Scales with                       |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Assistant turn (mean of 20 real questions)          | 18,878 / 207, 2.25 model calls     | **US$0.0025** (max 0.0047)   | Questions asked                   |
| Proposal pass, Decision call (9-source batch)       | 2,430 / 355                        | US$0.00058                   | New source text                   |

The second model call added in #114 (Tasks and Milestones) raises the cost of a pass by 84%, not 100%, because its prompt leaves out the Conversation and it writes less.
It is still cheap because both calls bill only new text.

## How cost scales with usage

Monthly inference per User is roughly `22 working days x (turns a day x US$0.0025 + saves a day x US$0.0004)`.
The Assistant dominates: one question costs about as much as six Evidence saves.

| User profile                           | Turns a day     | Saves a day | Inference a month | Share of a US$12 seat |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------- | ----------- | ----------------- | --------------------- |
| Light                                  | 3               | 2           | US$0.18           | 1.5%                  |
| Typical active PM                      | 10              | 5           | US$0.59           | 5%                    |
| Heavy                                  | 30              | 15          | US$1.78           | 15%                   |
| At the daily cap, most expensive turns | 50 at US$0.0047 | 20          | US$5.35           | 45%                   |

What this means for pricing:

- **Typical use is cheap enough for a per-seat price.** Inference is about 5% of a Team seat, leaving room for hosting, storage, analytics and support.
- **The cap matters.** `ASSISTANT_DAILY_TURN_CAP` (50) bounds the Assistant at about US$0.13 a day at the mean turn cost and US$0.23 at the worst, so no single User can cost more than about US$5 a month. Nearly half a seat is too much for a paid tier at scale, so Team keeps a fair-use cap and usage above it becomes overage.
- **Proposal cost is bounded by content, not activity.** Re-saving, editing without new text and repeat passes cost nothing, so a PM cannot run up the bill by using the product more, only by adding more material.
- **Caching is the next lever.** Only 29% of Assistant prompt tokens were served from cache on direct OpenAI, against 95% through OpenRouter on 28 September for the same model. The Project summary is re-sent every step; improving cache reuse or trimming it would cut the dominant cost.
- **Model choice is a lever in both directions.** The 28 September bake-off measured `gemini-2.5-flash` at US$0.0018 a turn and Claude Haiku at US$0.0128 (M9). A tier that lets Users pick an expensive model needs a surcharge; bring-your-own-key moves inference to the customer's bill entirely.

Fixed platform costs (Vercel hosting, Neon Postgres, Blob storage, PostHog) do not grow per User until storage and compute volume do, and are shared across every seat.
The research preview runs within their entry-level allowances; we would re-measure against paid plans before fixing prices.

## Why this model fits

- A per-user team subscription is familiar to project-management buyers (Jira and Notion, the two product alternatives in M1, both price per seat) and aligns revenue with the number of people receiving collaboration value.
- The free Preview supports product-led adoption: the value only shows once a Project has Evidence and Decisions in it, so a User must be able to try it on a real Project before paying.
- Higher tiers are justified by collaboration, governance and integrations rather than by hiding core Project data behind a paywall.
- Inference scales with usage, so the Preview carries fair-use caps (the daily turn cap already exists in code) rather than unlimited AI.
- Bring-your-own-key already ships (ADR 0011): a team on its own provider account moves inference cost off our books, and privacy-sensitive teams keep the model relationship in their control.
  That makes a lower "BYO key" seat price possible later without risking margin.
- The most important finding for pricing came from the model bake-off: model choice is an 8x cost lever for similar quality (M12).
  Pricing must assume a cache-friendly model; a tier that let every User pick an expensive, uncached model would need a usage surcharge.

## Factors that influenced the decision

| Factor          | How it shaped pricing                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Production cost | Measured inference of about US$0.59 per typical User per month (about US$5 at the cap) makes a seat price viable; storage and Postgres scale with Evidence volume, so large-file tiers are where limits belong |
| Perceived value | The value is time not spent reconstructing why something was decided during a handover; one saved hour a month exceeds the seat price for any PM                                                               |
| Competition     | Anchored near the per-seat price of Jira and Notion paid plans rather than above them; we compete on memory, not on more views, so we cannot charge a premium over incumbents until the memory is proven       |
| AI inference    | Measured per unit on current main, capped per User per day, billed only on new text for Proposals, and removable through BYO key                                                                               |
| Trust           | We would never sell Evidence or Project content; the moat is trust and accumulated memory (M3), so no revenue stream may depend on the data itself                                                             |

## Revenue streams

1. Per-seat subscriptions (primary).
2. Usage-based overage for heavy Assistant or Render use above the tier cap.
3. Paid integrations and importers (Jira, meeting platforms).
4. Migration and onboarding services for teams moving an existing decision log in.
5. Enterprise support and private or vendor-direct model deployment.
