# Milestone 1 - Competitors

## Framing

These are **the three closest alternatives available to our target user**, a Project Manager on a recurring or context-heavy Project who wants to keep the reasoning behind Decisions.
Two are products (Jira with Rovo, Notion with Notion AI).
The third is the workaround most teams actually use today: keeping files in a shared repository and asking a general-purpose LLM about them.
It is not a single product, but it is what we most often have to displace, so we compare against it directly.

What each alternative offers is taken from its vendor's public documentation, checked on 30 September 2026 and linked inline.
We did not test the paid AI tiers hands-on, so a cell below says what the documentation describes, not what we measured, and these products change monthly.

## 1. Jira + Rovo

**Pros**

- The default tool for software project execution: issues, workflows, sprints, dependencies and reporting at scale.
- Rovo adds AI search, chat and agents across Jira, Confluence and connected tools, so a PM can ask questions over the whole Atlassian estate.
- Rovo already turns meetings into proposed changes a person reviews: after a Loom recording it "maps the decisions and action items to the actual work items" and "surfaces a batch of suggested updates" that a team member applies or dismisses ([Jira launch note, 27 July 2026](https://jirareleases.atlassian.com/announcements/your-meeting-decisions-already-updated-in-jira)); its "Create action plan" skill reads meeting notes and writes a page of "decisions, action items, owners, and open questions" ([Rovo skills](https://support.atlassian.com/rovo/docs/add-skills-to-rovo-agents/)).
- Confluence has a Decision template and a per-space decision log ([Decisions blueprint](https://confluence.atlassian.com/doc/decisions-blueprint-339739406.html)).
- Deep ecosystem, permissions and enterprise adoption.

**Cons**

- In the documentation above, an extracted decision becomes an update to a work item or a line on a Confluence page; we found no documented structure for the conditions a Decision rests on or for which Decision replaced it.
- Rovo answers are drawn from whatever it can search, and we found nothing documenting a way to restrict a "why" answer to confirmed decisions, so a proposal the team rejected can come back as "the reason".
- We found no documented feature that flags earlier decisions when a date or a person they depended on changes.

## 2. Notion + Notion AI

**Pros**

- Flexible pages and databases; teams can build a decision-log template alongside their project tracker.
- Notion AI Meeting Notes transcribe a meeting and produce "instant summaries, key decisions, and action items with clear owners" ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/product/ai/use-cases/make-your-meetings-more-actionable)).
- Notion AI answers questions over the workspace and links to the pages it used.
- Low friction and widely adopted by small teams and student groups.

**Cons**

- The decisions Meeting Notes find are part of a summary; the page does not describe them becoming records in a decision log that someone confirms one by one.
- The schema is whatever the team built, so Assumptions, supersession and links to affected work are optional and not checked.
- Answers draw on every page, so a draft or an abandoned idea carries the same weight as a confirmed Decision unless the team curates what the AI can see.

## 3. Shared repository + a general-purpose LLM (NotebookLM, Gemini or ChatGPT)

**Pros**

- Free or already paid for, and no new tool to adopt.
- Grounded tools such as NotebookLM answer only from the uploaded sources, with inline citations to the passage behind each claim.
- Strong at summarising long transcripts and documents on demand.

**Cons**

- It answers from text, not from a record of what the team agreed, so a Decision and a suggestion made in the same meeting look alike.
- Nothing is persisted as structured data: no Decision, Assumption or supersession exists until someone asks, and the answer can differ each time.
- It is separate from where the work is tracked, so it cannot connect a changed date to the Decisions and Tasks that depend on it, or act on the Project.

## Summary

"Not documented" means we found no such feature in the vendor documentation linked above, not that it cannot be built with templates, automations or custom agents.

|                                                | Jira + Rovo                                          | Notion + Notion AI                   | Repository + LLM | PrismPM |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------- | ------- |
| Task and project execution                     | Strongest                                            | Good                                 | None             | Good    |
| Decisions as records                           | Confluence Decision pages and log                    | Manual template                      | No               | Yes     |
| Decisions proposed from notes, human-confirmed | Yes, as suggested work-item updates (Loom) or a page | In a meeting summary, not as records | No               | Yes     |
| "Why" answered only from confirmed Decisions   | Not documented                                       | Not documented                       | No               | Yes     |
| Explicit Assumptions and supersession          | Not documented                                       | If the team adds it                  | No               | Yes     |
| Review triggered when an Assumption breaks     | Not documented                                       | Not documented                       | No               | Yes     |

## Where PrismPM is better, stated literally

**PrismPM is not better at generic project execution.**
Jira has more workflow depth and Notion is more flexible; we do not try to match either.
Proposing decisions from meeting notes is not unique either: Rovo already does it for Jira work items.

PrismPM is designed to be better at a narrower job: **preserving human-confirmed rationale as structured records**.
Concretely, it does four things together that we did not find documented for any of the three alternatives.

1. **Answers only from confirmed rationale.** Decisions are proposed from Evidence with the exact sentence they came from, and enter the record only when a PM accepts them. "Why did we...?" is answered only from those confirmed Decisions, with a citation, or with "There is no recorded decision about that." (M10, M11).
2. **Explicit Assumptions.** Each Decision records the conditions it rests on, such as a Milestone date or a named Person, as structured data rather than prose.
3. **Supersession.** When a Decision replaces an earlier one, the link is recorded, and an answer about the old Decision names its replacement (eval case `w03`).
4. **Change-triggered review.** When a Project fact changes and contradicts an Assumption, PrismPM raises an alert naming the downstream Decisions to revisit.

For a PM taking over a Project mid-way, that is the difference between reading months of notes and asking one question.
