# Milestone 1 - Competitors

## Framing

These are **the three closest alternatives available to our target user**, a Project Manager on a recurring or context-heavy Project who wants to keep the reasoning behind Decisions.
Two are products (Jira with Rovo, Notion with Notion AI).
The third is the workaround most teams actually use today: keeping files in a shared repository and asking a general-purpose LLM about them.
It is not a single product, but it is what we most often have to displace, so we compare against it directly.

## 1. Jira + Rovo

**Pros**

- The default tool for software project execution: issues, workflows, sprints, dependencies and reporting at scale.
- Rovo adds AI search, chat and agents across Jira, Confluence and connected tools, so a PM can ask questions over the whole Atlassian estate.
- Deep ecosystem, permissions and enterprise adoption.

**Cons**

- Rationale lives in issue comments, Confluence pages and linked documents; there is no first-class Decision record with its alternatives, Assumptions and the Decision that replaced it.
- An AI answer is drawn from whatever it can search, confirmed or not, so a proposal someone floated and the team rejected can come back as "the reason".
- Nothing flags which earlier choices depended on a date or a person when that fact changes.

## 2. Notion + Notion AI

**Pros**

- Flexible pages and databases; teams can build a decision-log template alongside their project tracker.
- Notion AI answers questions over the workspace, summarises meeting notes and links to the pages it used.
- Low friction and widely adopted by small teams and student groups.

**Cons**

- A decision log is only as good as the discipline to fill it in; the template does not extract Decisions from notes for someone to confirm.
- The schema is whatever the team built, so Assumptions, supersession and links to affected work are optional, inconsistent and not checked.
- Answers draw on every page, so a draft or an abandoned idea carries the same weight as a confirmed Decision.

## 3. Shared repository + a general-purpose LLM (NotebookLM, Gemini or ChatGPT)

**Pros**

- Free or already paid for, and no new tool to adopt.
- Grounded tools such as NotebookLM answer with citations to the uploaded sources.
- Strong at summarising long transcripts and documents on demand.

**Cons**

- It answers from text, not from a record of what the team agreed; it cannot tell a Decision from a suggestion in the same meeting.
- Nothing is persisted as structured data: no Decision, Assumption or supersession exists until someone asks, and the answer can differ each time.
- It is separate from where the work is tracked, so it cannot connect a changed date to the Decisions and Tasks that depend on it, or act on the Project.

## Summary

|                                                | Jira + Rovo | Notion + Notion AI  | Repository + LLM | PrismPM |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------- | ---------------- | ------- |
| Task and project execution                     | Strongest   | Good                | None             | Good    |
| Decisions as first-class records               | No          | Manual template     | No               | Yes     |
| Decisions proposed from notes, human-confirmed | No          | No                  | No               | Yes     |
| "Why" answered only from confirmed Decisions   | No          | No                  | No               | Yes     |
| Explicit Assumptions and supersession          | No          | If the team adds it | No               | Yes     |
| Review triggered when an Assumption breaks     | No          | No                  | No               | Yes     |

## Where PrismPM is better, stated literally

**PrismPM is not better at generic project execution.**
Jira has more workflow depth and Notion is more flexible; we do not try to match either.

PrismPM is designed to be better at a narrower job: **preserving human-confirmed rationale**.
Concretely, it does four things none of the three alternatives does.

1. **Human-confirmed rationale.** Decisions are proposed from Evidence with the exact sentence they came from, and enter the record only when a PM accepts them. "Why did we...?" is answered only from those confirmed Decisions, with a citation, or with "There is no recorded decision about that." (M10, M11).
2. **Explicit Assumptions.** Each Decision records the conditions it rests on, such as a Milestone date or a named Person, as structured data rather than prose.
3. **Supersession.** When a Decision replaces an earlier one, the link is recorded, and an answer about the old Decision names its replacement (eval case `w03`).
4. **Change-triggered review.** When a Project fact changes and contradicts an Assumption, PrismPM raises an alert naming the downstream Decisions to revisit.

For a PM taking over a Project mid-way, that is the difference between reading months of notes and asking one question.
